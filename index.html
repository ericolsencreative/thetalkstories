TOLD BY A MAN · RAISED OTHER CHRISTIAN

The Kitchen Counter

In sixth grade, a sex-ed group came in to talk with our class. We had to bring a note home to our parents informing them we had been taught about sex in school and that this might be a good time to add any supplementary information for the questions your kid has. I gave the note to my Mom when I got home and she told me that my Dad would talk to me about it when he got home. My Dad got home, read the note, and stared at me. He picked me up and sat me on the kitchen counter. There was complete silence for what felt like hours, but was actually at least 15 minutes. Eventually my dad looked at me, took a deep breath, and gave me the only 5 words of sexual education I’ve ever gotten from my father: “Keep it in your pants.

WHAT I WANTED

A bit more substance would have been nice. Substance on anything, really.

TOLD BY A MAN · RAISED PROTESTANT

My father picked me up from basketball practice when I was 13 years old. The car ride home was strangely quiet and uncomfortable. I became nervous he was about to tell me that my parents were getting a divorce. Then, 2 minutes from home, he turns to me and slowly says, "...do you know what a scrotum is?" Stunned, I quietly said, "...I think so...". I didn't. He then responded biologically with, "It's your ball sack." And we finished the ride home in complete and utter silence. I can only imagine he was guilted into having this conversation by my mother whom he later told, 'Yup, I did it! We had the talk. Went great.'

WHAT I WANTED

Sex is good.

TOLD BY A WOMAN · RAISED OTHER CHRISTIAN

When I was in 3rd grade, my mother let my older sister and I stay up past bedtime so she and my dad could talk to us after my younger brothers went to bed. She handed us each a book to be read alone and in private, to hide them from our younger brothers, and to save any questions for the following night. My sister and I were to swap our books when finished, as though the other book had mysteriously left something out. They did not. The serious and almost angry look on my mom’s face did not match her words about how sex was a “beautiful thing”. I also couldn’t believe that boys had parts dangling around in the pants. My parents’ attempt at openness didn’t coincide with the abstinence-only policy they pushed.

WHAT I WANTED

Sex is completely normal and a part of the human experience. If you have any questions about it, come to me first. There’s a lot of bad information out there. I’ll be happy to answer them and help you feel safe and ready when you’re ready to start having sex.

TOLD BY A WOMAN · RAISED CATHOLIC

In catholic school, we spent a week in 7th grade on ‘family life education’. The two main takeaways that stuck with me were 1) Girls who don’t cross their legs end up pregnant, and 2) If you have sex, you will get pregnant and die. I was left feeling utterly confused as to how sex actually worked, but newly devoted to how to comfortably cross my legs at all times.

WHAT I WANTED

The basic biological information of how sex works.

I stumbled across a kid’s book that explained the biological steps necessary to produce pregnancy in pretty straightforward language. Why we asked

We have kids, and we wanted to know how to talk to them about sex. When we asked friends for advice, we learned that most of the talks we got were vague, scary, or never happened. So we asked a lot more people.
