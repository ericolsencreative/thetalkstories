TOLD BY A MAN · RAISED OTHER CHRISTIAN

The Kitchen Counter

In sixth grade, a sex-ed group came in to talk with our class. We had to bring a note home to our parents informing them we had been taught about sex in school and that this might be a good time to add any supplementary information for the questions your kid has. I gave the note to my Mom when I got home and she told me that my Dad would talk to me about it when he got home. My Dad got home, read the note, and stared at me. He picked me up and sat me on the kitchen counter. There was complete silence for what felt like hours, but was actually at least 15 minutes. Eventually my dad looked at me, took a deep breath, and gave me the only 5 words of sexual education I’ve ever gotten from my father: “Keep it in your pants.

WHAT I WANTED

A bit more substance would have been nice. Substance on anything, really.

TOLD BY A MAN · RAISED PROTESTANT

I became nervous he was about to tell me that my parents were getting a divorce. Then, 2 minutes from home, he turns to me and slowly says, "...do you know what a scrotum is?" Stunned, I quietly said, "...I think so...". I didn't. He then responded biologically with, "It's your ball sack." And we finished the ride home in complete and utter silence. I can only imagine he was guilted into having this conversation by my mother whom he later told, 'Yup, I did it! We had the talk. Went great.'

TOLD BY A WOMAN · RAISED OTHER CHRISTIAN

She handed us each a book to be read alone and in private, to hide them from our younger brothers, and to save any questions for the following night. My sister and I were to swap our books when finished, as though the other book had mysteriously left something out. They did not. The serious and almost angry look on my mom’s face did not match her words about how sex was a “beautiful thing”. I also couldn’t believe that boys had parts dangling around in the pants. Why we asked

We have kids, and we wanted to know how to talk to them about sex. When we asked friends for advice, we learned that most of the talks we got were vague, scary, or never happened. So we asked a lot more people.
